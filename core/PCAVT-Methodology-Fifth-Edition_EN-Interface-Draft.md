PCAVT inherits the no-ontology position of the Fourth Edition and reinforces it in the Fifth Edition as the starting point of all argumentation.

The core question of ontology is "what is the essence of the world" — matter? spirit? process? This tradition of questioning has continued for 2,500 years, from Thales to Heidegger. PCAVT's ruling on this question is procedural: **all ontological propositions automatically fail at the level of validity.**

Now apply this requirement to ontological propositions:

- "The essence of the world is matter" — how to verify? Any phenomenon you observe can be explained as "some manifestation of matter." The proposition provides no conceivable observation that could negate it.

- "The essence of the world is process" — likewise.

**Conclusion: all ontological propositions, without exception, are unfalsifiable.** By Popper's demarcation criterion, they are not scientific propositions; by the First Law's ruling criterion, they automatically fail at the level of pursuing validity.

This is not to say that ontological propositions are "wrong" — "wrong" itself is a judgment requiring a verification scheme. Rather: ontological propositions, at the level of pursuing validity, produce no verifiable cognitive increment. PCAVT does not answer "what is the world" — it answers only one operational question: **"What problem is the practice agent stuck on?"**

The answer is no. > **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section argues Theorem 0.2 (the smuggling mechanism).

The completion of the no-ontology declaration raises a deeper question: if PCAVT rejects all ontological propositions, then do the very terms it uses — "唯物", "实践", "异化" — also carry ontological residue? Where do these terms come from? Are their meanings reliable?

This question points to a phenomenon never systematically examined before, which this paper calls **semantic smuggling**.

#### 0.2.1 Two Types of Symbol Systems

The Self-Reference Consistency Statement has already established two structural types of symbol systems:

**Physically-grounded symbol system (Chinese).** In the generation of the symbol's form, part of the observable features of its referent in Layer A is preserved. The physical form of the symbol itself carries residual information about its referent. The character 河 (hé, "river") has the 氵 (water) radical directly pointing to the water class; 休 (xiū, "rest") is structured as 人 leaning on 木 (a person leaning on a tree); 日 (rì, "sun") in oracle-bone script was once literally the shape of the sun. The conventional meanings of Chinese — i.e., the meanings native speakers default to in daily use — are anchored on A-layer residue, not dependent on any theoretical framework's coloring. A Chinese native speaker who has never studied philosophy says "实践" (shijian, "practice"), and what he means is "实在地去做了" ("actually went and did it") — this is the literal meaning itself, unrelated to any philosophical tradition.

**Arbitrary symbol system (pinyin languages).** The relation between the symbol's form and its referent is purely conventional. The symbol carries no A-layer information about its referent. "water" is connected to water only by convention; the word-formation of "materialism" (material + ism) provides no physical information about "matter." For arbitrary systems, word meaning is entirely determined by usage history — a word's meaning is the sum total of its entire history of use within theoretical traditions.

#### 0.2.2 The Definition of Semantic Smuggling

**Semantic smuggling: when a theoretical term from a pinyin language is translated into Chinese, the semantic load accumulated in its pinyin-language theoretical tradition (framework coloring) attempts, through translation, to enter the conventional meaning of the Chinese term.**

The smuggling mechanism is as follows: the meaning of a pinyin-language term (e.g., materialism) is essentially a precipitate of an ontological framework — it accumulated the ontological assertion "the essence of the world is matter" in the theories of its users such as Hobbes, La Mettrie, and Marx. When materialism is translated as "唯物" (weiwu, "only matter"), the translator faces a choice: take the literal meaning of Chinese "唯物" ("only matter" — a plain realist attitude), or take the framework meaning of materialism ("the essence of the world is matter" — an ontological assertion)?

In the overwhelming majority of translation practice, the latter is adopted by default — because the translator himself lives within the framework-colored tradition and is unaware of the difference between the literal meaning of "唯物" and the framework meaning of materialism. Thus the ontological assertion, through translation, silently enters the default understanding of the Chinese term.

#### 0.2.3 Why Overturning Ontology = Canceling the Source of Smuggling

PCAVT overturns ontology — all ontological propositions automatically fail at the level of validity. And the framework coloring of pinyin terms comes precisely from ontological frameworks.

- The ontological framework is overturned → the source of coloring deposited on pinyin terms is canceled
- The meaning of the pinyin term (anchored to the overturned framework) → consequently fails
- The meaning of the Chinese term (anchored to A-layer residue) → unaffected, continues to hold

**Overturning ontology = canceling the coloring source of pinyin terms.** Chinese terms are independent of frameworks because their meanings are anchored in literal form (A-layer residue), not anchored in any theoretical framework.

#### 0.2.4 Rebuttal: "Japanese Also Has Kanji"

A natural objection: Japanese also uses kanji (Chinese characters). Why is Japanese not a physically-grounded symbol system?

The rebuttal is direct: **Japanese is not Chinese; merely using Chinese characters does not constitute Chinese.** The physical groundedness of Chinese characters (A-layer residue) only takes effect within the Chinese language system — the complete structure of character meanings, pictophonetic and associative composition (形声/会意), and a 3,000-year tradition of conventional meaning. Japanese borrowed the characters but did not inherit the Chinese language system: in Japanese, characters are doubly digested through on'yomi (音读) and kun'yomi (训读) readings (生 can be read せい, しょう, いきる, うまれる, なま — five readings), the same character form points to completely different objects in different contexts, and the A-layer anchoring is thoroughly diluted by the phonological system. Borrowing a symbol is not inheriting a system.

The key of this rebuttal: physical groundedness is not "a property of the character form," but "a property of the entire language system." Chinese characters are physically grounded in the Chinese system, not in the Japanese system — because the structures of the systems differ.

### 0.2.5 Reductio of the Language-Perfection Presupposition

> **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section is a reductio argument.
> Formalization: let P = "language L is perfect (word meanings precise, no verification needed)." Reductio: if P is true, then ∀ evolution E(L) (lexical/grammatical/semantic change), E(L) is unnecessary (perfection needs no change). But the empirical fact: L continuously evolves (L_Greek → L_Latin → L_English-German-French), i.e., ∃E(L) has occurred. Evolution is the admission that "the old form is imperfect" ⇒ ¬P. Contradiction ⇒ P is false.
> Proof strategy: reductio — assume perfection ⇒ derive "language should cease evolving" ⇒ contradicts the fact of "language continuously evolving" ⇒ the perfection presupposition is false.

Semantic smuggling operates unimpeded in pinyin-language translation because of a deep presupposition supporting it: **Western philosophy by default treats its language/writing as a perfect carrier** — word meanings reliable, no verification needed. This presupposition is the "safe house" of semantic smuggling: if language itself is perfect, then word meanings are naturally reliable, and no review of the source of meaning is required.

This presupposition cannot survive a simple reductio.

#### Reductio: If Language Were Perfect, Language Should Have Remained in the Stone Age

**If a language were perfect — its vocabulary, grammar, and semantics all precisely correct — then in theory there is no reason for it to change.** Every evolution of a language — vocabulary renewal, grammatical change, semantic drift — is an admission that "the old form is imperfect." Why would you change a perfect thing?

- Vocabulary renewal (e.g., the appearance of "手机" shouji / "cell phone" and "互联网" hulianwang / "internet"): the old vocabulary could not express new things — the old vocabulary was imperfect
- Grammatical change (e.g., the morphological simplification of English): the old grammar was inefficient — the old grammar was imperfect
- Semantic drift (e.g., "gay" changing from "cheerful" to "homosexual"): the old semantics caused confusion — the old semantics was imperfect

**The continuous evolution of language is the continuous evidence of language's imperfection.** And the languages of Western philosophy — from Greek to Latin to English-German-French — have continuously evolved over thousands of years and never stopped.

### The Fact of Western Philosophy: Language Keeps Changing; the History of Theory Is the Evidence

The entire theoretical history of Western philosophy is precisely the greatest evidence of its language's imperfection:

- Aristotle's Greek philosophy → replaced by scholastic Latin → replaced by modern English-German-French philosophy
- Every "replacement" is a paradigm revolution (in Kuhn's sense) — and a paradigm revolution is precisely the admission that "the old theoretical framework is imperfect"
- If the language and theory of Western philosophy were perfect, why has it changed countless times in 2,500 years?

**Western philosophy writes in a continuously evolving language while defaulting to language as a perfect carrier — this is self-contradiction.**

### The Structure of the Self-Contradiction

The semantic-smuggling paradigm of Western philosophy — maintaining word meanings with framework coloring, protecting meanings from review with the false presupposition of "language perfection" — rests on two mutually conflicting facts:

1. **Presupposition:** language is perfect (meanings reliable, no verification needed)
2. **Fact:** language continuously evolves (meanings drift with usage history; framework coloring exists)

Presupposition and fact conflict. Semantic smuggling exploits precisely this conflict: when "meanings must be reliable" is needed (when defending one's own terms), it defaults to language perfection; when "meanings must be flexible" is needed (when introducing new concepts), it exploits framework coloring.

### Both Roads Fail

Facing the reductio, Western philosophy has only two roads:

- **Admit language is imperfect** → then meanings may carry erroneous presuppositions → must accept verification → return to the First Law (the verification standard consistent with PCAVT)
- **Insist language is perfect** → then language should have remained in the Stone Age (absurd — because Western philosophy's own language keeps changing)

Both roads lead to the failure of Western philosophy. The first road means Western philosophy must accept the First Law's verification — this is precisely PCAVT's position; the second road is logical absurdity and cannot stand.

### Preventive Rebuttal: "Analytic Philosophy Is Improving Language"

A possible rebuttal: analytic philosophy (Frege, Russell, Wittgenstein) is precisely "improving language" — they built formal languages (concept-writing, predicate logic). Isn't that admitting language is imperfect and trying to solve it?

This rebuttal precisely proves this paper's point, rather than negating it:

- The formal-language attempt of analytic philosophy **precisely proves that Western philosophy knows natural language is imperfect** — if they didn't know, they would not labor to build formal languages
- But their solution is **building another formal language**, not **verifying the presuppositions of natural language** — they circumvented natural language, avoiding the review of the source of natural-language meanings
- **Circumvention ≠ solution.** Building a new language to replace the old one equals admitting the old language has presupposition problems but refusing to face them — this is worse than not admitting, because it uses a "technical solution" to mask the "presupposition problem"

Therefore, the formal-language attempt of analytic philosophy instead supports PCAVT's thesis: Western philosophy admits language has problems, but its solution (circumventing natural language) avoids presupposition verification rather than confronting it.

### 0.5 The Concept-Reference Filter

The core feature of philosophy is dependence on conceptual argumentation — obtaining conclusions through conceptual analysis, inference, and system construction. Mathematics likewise lacks this feature:

Therefore, mathematics' evaluation system is not subordinate to philosophy. Philosophy relies on conceptual argumentation (contestable, undecidable); mathematics relies on formal proof (incontestable, decidable).

Combining the above two points: mathematics does not belong to science (does not rely on empirical verification), does not belong to philosophy (does not rely on conceptual argumentation) — **mathematics is self-sufficient.** Its validity standard (formal proof) differs from both science (empirical verification) and philosophy (conceptual argumentation).

### 2.4.2 The First Law: Verifiability (the Practice-Anchored Razor)

**Statement:** any cognition aiming to understand or intervene in reality must be able to convert its core propositions into a practice-verification scheme completable in finitely many steps with publicly observable results.

**Derivation:** directly from Ω's operation — the physical interface requires verification to anchor to physical causality. The First Law generalizes this requirement into a universal discipline:

1. Verification must be publicly observable (otherwise it cannot anchor to physical causality — an observer's personal experience cannot be publicly cross-checked)
2. Verification must have finitely many steps (otherwise it cannot be completed — an infinite-step verification is not executable)
3. Verification must be convertible into a practical scheme (otherwise it stays in theory — not grounded means not practical verification)

**Function:** the First Law is the only master law — it prescribes the validity criterion of all claims (verifiability). The other two laws are its unfolding.

### 2.4.3 The Second Law: Framework Reflexivity (the Cognitive Self-Reflexive Razor)

**Statement:** any cognitive framework must contain the path of its own falsification.

**Derivation:** the First Law requires "claims verifiable." If a framework can never be falsified (accepts no refutation), then all its claims about itself are unverifiable — it becomes a self-enclosed absolute. The Second Law generalizes this requirement to the framework level:

1. A framework must contain the path of its own falsification (otherwise the framework is unverifiable — it is forever "correct" because it refuses verification)
2. The falsification path of a framework must be operational (cannot be a hollow statement like "I would be wrong if the world were destroyed")
3. Correction of the framework must be allowed (the Second Law does not permit "uncorrectable frameworks" to exist)

**Function:** the Second Law prevents cognitive frameworks from self-absolutizing — any framework (including PCAVT itself) must retain the possibility of being falsified. This is the First Law's unfolding in the temporal dimension: claims verifiable now, frameworks falsifiable in the future.

### 2.4.4 The Third Law: Rule Self-Adjudication (the Coordination Self-Adjudicating Razor)

**Statement:** any coordination rule must contain the channel of its own challenge.

**Derivation:** the First Law requires "claims verifiable." If a rule (institution, law, social norm) does not accept challenge (cannot be questioned, cannot be modified), then its claim about its own legitimacy is unverifiable — it becomes a self-enclosed absolute. The Third Law generalizes this requirement to the rule level:

1. A rule must contain the channel of its own challenge (otherwise the rule is unverifiable — it is forever "legal" because it refuses challenge)
2. The challenge channel must be operational (cannot be "you may protest" while protest is ineffective — that is a hollow channel)
3. Modification of the rule must be allowed (the Third Law does not permit "unmodifiable rules" to exist)

**Function:** the Third Law prevents coordination rules from self-absolutizing — any rule must retain the possibility of being challenged. This is the First Law's unfolding in the social dimension: claims verifiable now, rules challengeable in the future.

### 2.4.5 The Unified Structure of the Three Laws

The Three Laws share one deep structure: **refusing verification = invalidity.**

- The First Law: a claim refuses verification → the claim fails (unverifiable claim)
- The Second Law: a framework refuses verification (refuses falsification) → the framework fails (self-enclosed framework)

### 3.4.1 The Regular Loop: Practice Initiation → Feedback → Verification → Correction

The regular interaction of the A-B-C three layers is a continuous loop:

> **Practice initiation → feedback → verification → correction → practice again**

4. **Correction:** verification results lead to correction — B1 cognitive framework updated, B2 coordination rules adjusted, C2 behavior modes improved
5. **Practice again:** the corrected C initiates practice again — the loop continues

**Features of the loop:**

- It is continuous (practice does not stop — stopping practice = stopping existence)
- It is spiral (each loop is based on the previous correction — not running in place)
- It is verification-driven (verification is the engine of the loop — without verification there is no correction)

### 3.4.2 The Role of the Three Laws in the Loop

The Three Laws play their roles at different stages of the loop:

- **The First Law (verifiability):** the claims of feedback must be verifiable — the core criterion of the verification stage

- **The Third Law (rule self-adjudication):** B2 coordination rules must be challengeable — the constraint on rules in the correction stage

**Three situations where the loop does not operate:**

- Verification failure (claims unverifiable) → feedback cannot be evaluated → the loop idles
- Framework closure (B1 uncorrectable) → correction cannot proceed → the loop stalls
- Rule monopoly (B2 unchallengeable) → rules refuse correction → the loop is locked by rules

4. Verification (the grounding effect accepts verification — did the bridge bear? did the institution produce the expected outcomes?)

# Part Four: The Joint Operation of the Three Laws

## 4.1 Right-Responsibility Symmetry (The Core Principle the Three Laws Jointly Point To)

> **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section derives right-responsibility symmetry from Axioms 1-3.
> Formalization: power item P_claim (the claimant possesses the power to claim) ⇒ responsibility item R_verify (Axiom 1: must be verifiable); P_framework (power to hold a framework) ⇒ R_correct (Axiom 2: must be correctable); P_rule (power to execute a rule) ⇒ R_challenge (Axiom 3: must be challengeable). Right-responsibility symmetry = for every power item P, there exists a matching responsibility item R (P ⇒ R). Right-responsibility misalignment = ∃P with no matching R (power without responsibility — violating the corresponding axiom).
> Proof strategy: a direct corollary of the Three-Laws joint structure — every power is matched by one law to one responsibility; right-responsibility misalignment ⟺ violating the Three Laws ⟺ invalidity.

Part Three establishes the A-B-C three layers and the dynamic loop. Now we enter Part Four: how the Three Laws operate jointly. Viewed separately, the Three Laws are three disciplines (verifiable/correctable/challengeable); but when they operate jointly, they jointly point to one core principle — **right-responsibility symmetry**.

### 4.1.1 The Joint Structure of the Three Laws

The Three Laws are not three independent matters — they are three forms of the same verification discipline (Part Two §2.4):

- **The First Law governs verification:** claims must be verifiable (the verification responsibility lies with the claimant)
- **The Second Law governs correction:** frameworks must be correctable (the correction responsibility lies with the framework holder)
- **The Third Law governs challenge:** rules must be challengeable (the challenge responsibility lies with the rule executor)

### 4.1.2 The Derivation of Right-Responsibility Symmetry

Examining the Three Laws jointly yields a structural corollary: **power must match responsibility.**

The derivation process:

1. **The claimant possesses the power to claim** (can claim anything) — but the First Law requires him to bear the verification responsibility (claims must be verifiable)
2. **The framework holder possesses the power of the framework** (can hold any cognitive framework) — but the Second Law requires him to bear the correction responsibility (frameworks must be correctable)
3. **The rule executor possesses the power of the rule** (can execute any rule) — but the Third Law requires him to bear the challenge responsibility (rules must be challengeable)

**The joint structure of the Three Laws = every power must be matched by a responsibility.** When power and responsibility are misaligned, the Three Laws are violated:

- **Power without responsibility:** the claimant only claims without verifying (propaganda, brainwashing — First-Law violation)
- **Power without responsibility:** the framework holder only holds without correcting (dogma, closure — Second-Law violation)
- **Power without responsibility:** the rule executor only executes without being challengeable (dictatorship, monopoly — Third-Law violation)

### 4.1.3 Right-Responsibility Symmetry as a Methodological Principle

Right-responsibility symmetry is not a moral appeal — it is a structural corollary of the methodology:

- It is directly derived from the Three Laws (not an externally introduced moral principle)
- It is operational (whether power-responsibility are matched is testable)
- It applies at all levels (claimants/framework holders/rule executors — anyone possessing power must match it with responsibility)

**Right-responsibility symmetry is part of the methodology's body: the joint operation of the Three Laws necessarily points to it.**

### 4.1.4 The Verification Operation of Right-Responsibility Symmetry

As a methodological principle, right-responsibility symmetry's verification operation is:

> **For any power claim, verify whether its responsibility is matched.**

- Power to claim ("I know X") → verify the verification responsibility ("can you prove it?" — First Law)
- Framework power ("my theory is complete") → verify the correction responsibility ("is there a falsification path?" — Second Law)
- Rule power ("this rule cannot be changed") → verify the challenge responsibility ("is there a challenge channel?" — Third Law)

**Power-responsibility matched = healthy; power-responsibility misaligned = violating the Three Laws = invalidity.**

## 4.2 Negative-Feedback Dynamics (The Dynamization of the Three Laws)

> **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section defines the dynamic mechanism of the Three Laws' joint operation (negative feedback).
> Formalization: negative-feedback loop = deviation detection (Axiom 1: verification) → correction (Axioms 2/3: correction) → re-detection (Axiom 1 re-executed). Aggressive negative feedback = actively seeking counterexamples (Second-Law deepening: the framework actively walks its own falsification path P(F), rather than passively waiting). Loop failure = detection failure (Axiom 1 violation) / correction failure (Axiom 2 violation) / loop rupture (Axiom 3 violation) ⇒ positive feedback takes over ⇒ deviation amplified.
> Proof strategy: the Three-Laws joint = detector (Axiom 1) + corrector (Axioms 2/3) = negative-feedback loop; the aggressive form = the Second Law's active execution.

§4.1 establishes the core principle the Three Laws' joint operation points to (right-responsibility symmetry). Now we examine the **dynamic mechanism** of the Three Laws' joint operation — how they operate as a whole. The answer: negative feedback.

### 4.2.1 The Definition of Negative Feedback

**Negative feedback = the system's response to deviation is reverse (correcting the deviation), not forward (amplifying the deviation).**

In PCAVT, the joint operating mechanism of the Three Laws is negative feedback:

> **Verification → correction → re-verification**

- Claims accept verification (First Law)
- Verification finds deviation → correction (Second/Third Law)
- After correction, re-verify (First Law re-executed)

The essence of this loop is negative feedback: **deviation detected → system reverses and corrects → deviation reduced.** Its opposite is positive feedback (deviation amplified): claims do not accept verification → deviation not discovered → claims deviate more and more (error accumulation).

### 4.2.2 The Relation between Negative Feedback and the Three Laws

Negative feedback is not a fourth law beyond the Three Laws — it is the dynamic form of the Three Laws' joint operation:

- **The First Law provides the detector** (verification — discovering deviation)
- **The Second/Third Laws provide the corrector** (correction — correcting deviation)
- **The Three-Laws joint = detection + correction = a negative-feedback loop**

**Analogy:** the operation of a thermostat — temperature deviates from the setpoint (detection) → heat/cool (correction) → temperature returns to the setpoint (negative feedback). The Three Laws are the thermostat of cognitive and social systems.

### 4.2.3 Passive Negative Feedback vs. Aggressive Negative Feedback

Negative feedback has two forms; PCAVT requires the latter:

**Passive negative feedback (insufficient):** waiting for deviation to be discovered — correcting only when claims are verified. The problem with this form: deviation may accumulate continuously during the undiscovered period (errors go undiscovered for long periods).

**Aggressive negative feedback (required):** actively seeking evidence that could overturn oneself — not waiting for verification, actively seeking deviation. This is the deepening of the Second Law:

> **Aggressive negative feedback = actively seeking evidence that could overturn oneself, rather than passively waiting to be overturned.**

- The framework actively seeks counterexamples (rather than waiting for others to find them)
- Rules actively open challenge channels (rather than waiting for others to challenge)
- Claims actively expose verification schemes (rather than waiting for others to demand them)

**Aggressive negative feedback is the active form of the Second Law (framework reflexivity):** the Second Law requires "frameworks must contain falsification paths" — the passive form is "the path exists but waits for others to walk it"; the aggressive form is "actively walking that path."

### 4.2.4 The Forms of Negative-Feedback Failure

The negative-feedback loop can fail — the failure forms are precisely the forms in which the Three Laws are violated:

- **Detection failure:** claims unverifiable → deviation cannot be discovered (First-Law violation)
- **Correction failure:** frameworks uncorrectable → deviation cannot be corrected (Second-Law violation)
- **Loop rupture:** rules unchallengeable → correction blocked (Third-Law violation)

**Negative-feedback failure = positive feedback takes over = deviation amplified = error accumulation.** This is precisely the "loop not operating" (§3.4.2: idling/stalling/locking) expressed in dynamics.

### 4.2.5 Negative Feedback as the Methodological Core

Negative-feedback dynamics is the core of the methodology — it is not optional optimization but a necessity for the system's operation:

- Without negative feedback, cognition cannot correct errors (error accumulation)
- Without aggressive negative feedback, correction is lagging (deviation accumulates long-term)
- Negative feedback makes PCAVT a "self-correcting system" — this is its fundamental difference from closed systems (refusing verification, refusing correction, refusing challenge)

**PCAVT = a self-correcting system operating on aggressive negative feedback as its core.**

## 4.3 The Mechanical-Conduction Structure (A Description of the Mechanism of Three-Laws Failure)

> **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section describes the stress conduction when the Three Laws fail (diagnostic framework).
> Formalization: stress-conduction path = B2 rupture (Axiom-3 violation: rules unchallengeable) → B1 condemnation (cognition identifies the problem — Axiom-2 verification) → condemnation suppressed (challenge channel still blocked — Axiom 3 continuously violated) → stress sinks into A1/A2 (material-layer pressure accumulates: A2_f declines, entropy increases) → eruption (uncontrollable release). Diagnosis = identifying the conduction stage (observable signals: rules unchallengeable / condemnation suppressed / material-layer pressure accumulation); not predicting eruption (cannot be precisely predicted — Ψ: C is inexhaustible).
> Proof strategy: a path description of negative-feedback rupture — contrast of the normal loop (Axioms 1-3 operating) vs. the ruptured loop (Axiom 3 continuously violated).

§4.1 and §4.2 examine the normal operation of the Three Laws (right-responsibility symmetry, negative feedback). Now we examine the mechanism when the Three Laws **fail** — when the Three Laws are violated, how stress conducts through the system. This is used as a diagnostic framework (describing the failure mechanism), not expanded into social-policy recommendations.

### 4.3.1 The Stress-Conduction Path

When the Three Laws are violated (rules unchallengeable, frameworks uncorrectable, claims unverifiable), stress within the system conducts along a typical path:

> **B2 rupture → B1 condemnation → condemnation suppressed → stress sinks into Layer A → eruption**

**Step-by-step expansion:**

1. **B2 rupture:** the coordination rule (B2) violates the Third Law — the rule is unchallengeable (the institution blocks the challenge channel). The rule's injustice/inefficacy begins accumulating stress (the discontent of those oppressed by the rule).
2. **B1 condemnation:** the cognitive framework (B1) begins condemning — the oppressed's cognitive framework identifies the rule's problem ("this is unfair"). Stress transfers from the rule layer to the cognitive layer.
3. **Condemnation suppressed:** B2 continues violating the Third Law — condemnation is suppressed (the challenge channel remains blocked). Stress cannot be released through normal channels (the rule cannot be challenged).
4. **Stress sinks into Layer A:** after cognitive-layer condemnation is suppressed, stress sinks to the material layer (Layer A) — discontent accumulates as material-level pressure (production stagnation, increased consumption, system entropy increase).
5. **Eruption:** after stress accumulates on Layer A to a critical point, it erupts — released in an uncontrollable manner (production collapse, social unrest, violent succession).

### 4.3.2 The Essence of Mechanical Conduction

The essence of this conduction path is **the consequence of the negative-feedback loop being blocked**:

- Normal case: B2 violation → B1 condemnation → rule challenged → correction (negative feedback — deviation corrected)
- Failure case: B2 violation → B1 condemnation → condemnation suppressed → stress sinks → eruption (negative feedback ruptured — deviation cannot be corrected, ultimately released by eruption)

**Eruption is not sudden — it is the endpoint of stress conduction.** Every link in the conduction path (B2 rupture, condemnation suppression, stress sinking) is a sign of negative-feedback rupture and can be diagnosed in advance.

### 4.3.3 Use as a Diagnostic Framework

The position of the mechanical-conduction structure in the methodology is a **diagnostic framework**:

- **Use:** diagnosing when a system is in a stress-accumulation state (whether negative feedback is healthy)
- **Diagnosable signals:** B2 rupture (rules unchallengeable), condemnation suppressed (challenge channel blocked), stress sinking (material-layer pressure accumulation) — all observable signals
- **Not expanded:** this framework describes the failure mechanism; it does not provide social-policy recommendations (how to respond to eruption, how to design institutions — those are applied-level topics, not part of the methodology's body)

### 4.3.4 The Operationalization of Diagnosis

As a diagnostic framework, the operationalization steps of the mechanical-conduction structure are:

1. Check B2: are the rules challengeable? (Third Law) — if unchallengeable, enter alarm
2. Check B1: is condemnation allowed to be expressed? (Second Law) — if suppressed, enter alarm
3. Check Layer A: is stress accumulating on the material layer? (A2 flow declining, entropy increasing) — if accumulating, enter alarm
4. All three alarms simultaneously present → the system is in a pre-eruption state (stress conduction has reached the final stage)

**The purpose of diagnosis is not to predict eruption (cannot be precisely predicted), but to identify negative-feedback rupture — rupture can be repaired (restore the challenge channel); eruption cannot be precisely predicted but can be prevented (repair the rupture).**

### 4.3.5 Closing of Part Four

The argumentation chain of Part Four closes completely:

1. Right-responsibility symmetry (§4.1) — the Three Laws jointly point to the core principle: power must match responsibility
2. Negative-feedback dynamics (§4.2) — the dynamic mechanism of the Three Laws' joint operation: verification→correction→re-verification; aggressive negative feedback is the core
3. Mechanical-conduction structure (§4.3) — the mechanism description of Three-Laws failure: B2 rupture→B1 condemnation→condemnation suppressed→stress sinks into Layer A→eruption (diagnostic framework)

**Core conclusion: the normal joint operation of the Three Laws = right-responsibility symmetry + negative feedback (self-correcting system); the joint failure of the Three Laws = stress conduction (negative-feedback rupture → eruption). What the methodology provides is diagnosis (identifying whether negative feedback is ruptured), not policy (how to respond to eruption).**

## 5.2 The Dual-State Structure of the Four Alarms (Originally the Five Alarms)

> **Formal annotation (interface syntax):** This section defines the dual-state structure of the four alarms.
> Formalization: each mechanism M has a dual state — the healthy state H(M) (a component of practice) and the alarm state W(M) (a decoupling alarm). The W(M) determination criterion = whether M is detached from A1 anchoring. The four alarms: logical idling (W = self-referential closed loop — violating Axiom 1), unknown-unknowns (W = framework closure — violating Axiom 2), incommensurability (W = forced translation — violating Axiom 1 at the terminological level), asymmetry (W = power blocking the challenge channel — violating Axiom 3). Operational cases have a three-part structure: situation (when it appears) / identification signal (observable marker) / verification action (the verification execution of the corresponding axiom).
> Proof strategy: dual-state determination = anchoring-state detection (§5.1.3); alarm state ⟺ the concrete form of Three-Laws violation.

§5.1 establishes the neutral description of practice deviation (the distinction between health and decoupling lies in the anchoring state). Now we examine four concrete mechanisms — each has a **dual-state structure**: healthy state and alarm state. This is a restructuring of the Fourth Edition's "five alarms": each mechanism is no longer a mere alarm, but "a healthy component of practice + an alarm when decoupled."

### 5.2.1 The Overall Logic of the Dual-State Structure

**Dual-state structure = in the healthy state each mechanism is a component of practice; in the decoupled state it is an alarm.**

- **Healthy state:** the mechanism operates normally — it is part of practice (a component of cognition, coordination, verification)
- **Alarm state:** the mechanism operates in decoupled form — it becomes an alarm (signaling that the system is deviating from A-layer anchoring)

**The significance of the dual state:** it is not "these four things are bad and must be eliminated" — but "these four mechanisms themselves are components of healthy practice; when they enter the decoupled state, they are alarms of decoupling." Eliminating the mechanisms themselves (e.g., eliminating logic, eliminating cognition) is foolish; identifying the mechanisms' decoupled state is the methodology's task.

### 5.2.2 Alarm One: Logical Idling

**Normal state (healthy):** healthy symbolic operation — logic operating normally (inference, computation, argumentation — components of cognition).

**Alarm state (decoupled):** self-referential closed loop — logic pointing only at itself (inference for inference, argumentation for argumentation), no longer anchoring any A-layer content.

**Operational case (identification signal + verification action):**

- **Situation:** a theoretical system repeatedly elaborates concepts at meetings (argumentation more and more refined), but never points to any testable grounding.
- **Identification signal (observable):** does the system's argumentation chain terminate at a publicly observable physical outcome? — if the endpoint of the argumentation is another concept (concept→concept→concept) rather than a physical outcome, it is in the logical-idling alarm state.
- **Verification action:** pose the First-Law verification to the system: "can your core proposition be converted into a verification scheme with finitely many steps, publicly observable?" — if yes, healthy (back to practice); if no, confirm logical idling (self-referential closed loop).

**The significance of the alarm:** when logic begins self-referential closure (no longer anchoring Layer A), it is a decoupling alarm — signaling that cognition is detaching from practice.

### 5.2.3 Alarm Two: Unknown-Unknowns

**Normal state (healthy):** admitting the cognitive boundary — knowing that one does not know ("I don't know" is the bottom-line form of honesty-defense — §0.6.1).

**Alarm state (decoupled):** framework closure — not knowing that one does not know (pretending to know — the unknown-unknowns of §0.6.1).

**Operational case (identification signal + verification action):**

- **Situation:** when an authoritative framework faces a counterexample, its first reaction is "you haven't understood my theory" (rather than verifying the counterexample).
- **Identification signal (observable):** does the framework produce corrective actions when facing counterexamples? — if every counterexample is explained away ("this is not a counterexample, it is a misunderstanding") and the framework never self-corrects, it is in the unknown-unknowns alarm state.
- **Verification action:** pose the Second-Law verification to the framework holder: "does your framework contain a falsification path? Can you name one counterexample that would make you correct?" — if yes, healthy (admitting the boundary); if no, confirm unknown-unknowns (framework closure).

**The significance of the alarm:** when cognition begins pretending to know (framework closure), it is a decoupling alarm — signaling that the framework is losing its open-correction capability (Second-Law violation).

### 5.2.4 Alarm Three: Incommensurability

**Normal state (healthy):** honest translation responsibility — admitting the differences between frameworks, bearing the honest translation responsibility (truthfully stating differences, not forcibly equating).

**Alarm state (decoupled):** forced translation — ignoring framework differences, forcibly translating concept A of one framework into framework B (the concept is distorted).

**Operational case (identification signal + verification action):**
