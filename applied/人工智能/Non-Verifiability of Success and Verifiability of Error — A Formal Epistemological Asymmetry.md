## Abstract

We demonstrate a fundamental asymmetry in epistemic verification: material success cannot verify the correctness of a cognitive framework, but material failure can falsify its errors. This asymmetry is not a matter of degree—it is a category distinction. Success attribution requires a counterfactual control group that the material world does not provide (infinite enumeration, no experimental control, and unverifiable counterfactual conditionals jointly render positive verification logically impossible). Error attribution requires no counterfactual control—the causal chain from a cognitive defect to a material consequence is continuous and traceable at every step. We identify the root of this asymmetry in a "root category error"—the conflation of a statistical outcome with causal certification—which serves as the template for all specific category errors across domains (bloodline legitimacy, divine will, and success mythology). We further formalize this asymmetry using an independently derived mathematical framework in social-cognitive dynamics, showing that positive feedback channels are structurally blocked (diffusion without convergence) while negative feedback channels remain uniquely operable (damped convergence toward material truth). The framework therefore operates as a pure negative-feedback engine. We compare our account with Popperian falsificationism and show that, while operationally compatible, the two address different levels of analysis—propositional logic versus inter-layer verification direction.

**Keywords:** epistemic asymmetry; verifiability; category error; negative feedback; causal attribution; falsificationism

## 0. Terminology

This paper introduces a minimal set of terms to distinguish between layers of verification:

- **Material Layer (M-layer):** The physical world, independent of any person's beliefs. The terminal point of all verification—a bridge collapses regardless of what anyone says about it.
- **Cognitive Framework (C-layer):** An individual's system of beliefs, understanding, and judgment criteria. C-layer claims about the world must ultimately be tested against the M-layer.
- **Operational Verification:** A procedure that different, disinterested parties can independently execute, producing publicly observable results.
- **Root Category Error:** A new concept introduced and defined in Section 2—the conflation of "a statistical outcome occurred" with "the cognitive framework that accompanied it is correct."
- **Material Outcome:** A publicly measurable consequence in the physical world (wealth accumulated, bridge built, code compiled).

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem

The operational verifiability principle—roughly, "claims about the world must be testable against material evidence"—sets the direction of calibration for any cognitive framework: feedback from the material layer is the sole legitimate source of cognitive correction.

However, this principle says nothing about the *directionality* of test results. When a cognitive framework produces a material outcome through practice—whether that outcome is good or bad—are the two cases symmetric in their verifying power? In other words: to what extent does a success prove that your method is correct? To what extent does a failure prove that your method is incorrect?

Our core claim: **they are asymmetric in principle.** Material success cannot verify cognitive correctness. Material failure can falsify cognitive error. This is not a matter of insufficient evidence—not "success evidence isn't enough yet; collect more and it'll prove it." It is a category distinction. The causal attribution of success is logically incompletable. The causal attribution of error is physically traceable at every step.

### 1.2 Structure

Section 2 identifies and dissects the root category error—"success = correctness"—as the template for all specific category errors. Section 3 argues from three logical incompletabilities that success cannot verify. Section 4 argues from causal chain traceability that error can falsify. Section 5 formalizes this asymmetry using an independently derived mathematical framework from social-cognitive dynamics. Section 6 compares this account with Popperian falsificationism. Section 7 derives implications. Section 8 concludes.

## 2. "Success = Correctness"—The Root Category Error

### 2.1 The Unified Syntax of Category Errors

A category error is the logical operation of assigning a concept from one category to another category where it does not belong. In social cognition, the most typical category error is the confusion between the material layer and the cognitive layer—treating a material outcome as verification of a cognitive claim.

All such category errors share a unified syntactic template:

> **Because outcome X occurred in the material layer, cognitive claim Y is true.**

The template's key feature is the arrow—from material outcome directly to cognitive claim, with no traceable causal conduction chain in between. Both ends of the arrow are individually true (the material outcome did occur; the cognitive claim is indeed a statable proposition), but the arrow itself—the "because... therefore..." causal connection—does not hold.

This template produces instances across domains:

- **Bloodline narrative:** "Because this family persisted for centuries (material outcome), this family possesses natural qualification to rule (cognitive claim)."
- **Divine-will narrative:** "Because our army won the war (material outcome), God exists and has chosen us (cognitive claim)."
- **Success mythology:** "Because I achieved material success in life (material outcome), my thinking and methods are correct (cognitive claim)."

In each case, there is no continuous causal chain between the material outcome and the cognitive claim—bloodline continuity does not equal governing capacity, military victory does not equal divine intervention, material success does not equal methodological correctness. Yet in each case, the executor uses "success = correctness" as the default assumption to complete the jump.

**Bloodline: The Habsburg Jaw and Victorian Hemophilia.** The Spanish Habsburg dynasty maintained blood purity through eleven generations of uncle-niece and cousin marriages. The material result was physically undeniable: the last king, Carlos II (1661–1700), suffered from mandibular prognathism so severe he could not chew, along with intellectual disability, epilepsy, and infertility. In 2009, geneticists published a quantitative analysis of the Habsburg pedigree in *PLOS ONE* [1], calculating Carlos II's inbreeding coefficient at 0.254—meaning over a quarter of his alleles were homozygous identical, higher than the offspring of sibling incest (0.25). The pursuit of blood purity produced a physiological wreck, and that wreck was precisely the "purest-blooded" person—**pure bloodline was negatively correlated with ruling capacity.** Similarly, Britain's Queen Victoria was a hemophilia carrier. Driven by bloodline narrative (royals must marry royals to preserve "noble blood"), her nine children were married into the royal houses of Germany, Russia, and Spain. Result: hemophilia was introduced into the entire European royal gene pool. Bloodline was not a guarantee of legitimacy—it was a channel for accelerated transmission of genetic defects through the power class.

**Divine Will: The Crusades and the Conquest of the Americas.** Pope Urban II launched the First Crusade in 1095 under the banner "Deus vult" (God wills it). Crusaders massacred nearly all inhabitants upon capturing Jerusalem—chronicler William of Tyre recorded that the slaughter was so severe "the blood reached the horses' knees" [2]. Meanwhile, Saladin, recapturing Jerusalem in 1187, likewise claimed "the will of Allah"—but he pardoned the city's Christians. The identical divine-will narrative framework simultaneously accommodated massacre and mercy—because divine will is an independently unobservable variable. Its sole function as an explanatory variable is: whoever holds power decides. In the 16th century, Spanish conquistadors slaughtered and enslaved across the Americas under the official ideology of Catholic missionary duty. Historian Henry F. Dobyns [3] estimated that the indigenous population of the Americas fell from approximately 54 million to approximately 6 million within a century of Columbus's arrival—an extinction rate exceeding 90%. Every post-conquest thanksgiving Mass executed the same root category error: massacre and conquest succeeded (material outcome) → God blessed us (cognitive claim).

**Success Mythology: The Survivorship Bias.** The core logic of the success industry—the totality of businesses, media, and training built on the premise that "successful people can teach you to succeed"—is survivorship bias. During WWII, statistician Abraham Wald [4] studied the bullet-hole distribution on returning bombers and found that wings and fuselage were densely perforated while engines and fuel tanks were sparsely hit. Military intuition: "reinforce the areas with dense bullet holes." Wald pointed out: you are only seeing the planes that returned. Planes hit in the engines and fuel tanks never returned—those bullet holes are systematically absent from your sample. The correct strategy: reinforce the areas where you see no bullet holes. The data collection mechanism of the success industry operates identically: collect only the sample of successes, extract "success factors" from them, and never collect the sample of those who used the same methods and failed. No success author can answer a simple question: how many people used your method and failed? The question has no answer—because there is no control group.

The effectiveness of this template does not depend on whether it is explicitly stated. Most executors never examine "success = correctness" as a conscious proposition. Its power comes from being a cognitive factory default—in hundreds of thousands of years of evolution, "my actions produced a good outcome, therefore my action strategy is correct" was a sufficiently efficient approximation in the savanna that it became hardwired into the nervous system. In simple environments, it is a low-cost, high-efficiency heuristic. In complex civilization, it becomes catastrophic cognitive short-circuiting.

### 2.2 The Template as Root

The unified syntax above is not one specific category error—it is the **legitimacy condition** for all category errors. Without the default assumption "success = correctness," no specific category error could gain cognitive foothold, because the executor would be unable to answer the question "what is your evidence?"

"Success = correctness" provides the pseudo-evidence: using a material outcome as if it were evidence.

It is therefore the "root template" of category errors—defining both the syntactic structure (material outcome → cognitive claim) and the legitimacy source ("I have a good outcome, therefore I am correct") of all specific category errors.

### 2.3 Arrow Inversion with the Verifiability Principle

The root template and the verifiability principle stand in structural arrow opposition.

The verifiability principle says: cognitive frameworks accept material verification—the arrow points from the material layer to the cognitive layer; the material layer is the judge.

The root category error says: material good outcomes certify cognitive frameworks—the arrow jumps from the material layer to the cognitive layer; the material layer becomes the endorser.

The verifiability principle is "falsification-prioritized"—it asks "has my cognitive framework been overturned by the material layer?" The root category error is "verification-prioritized"—it asks "has my cognitive framework been proven by the material layer?"

## 3. Success Cannot Verify—Structural Fracture in Causal Attribution

### 3.1 Three Logical Incompletabilities

To prove "my method (C-layer) caused my success (material outcome)," the arguer must complete at least one of the following three operations. We demonstrate each is incompletable.

**First: Infinite Enumeration Is Incompletable.**

The causal space of material outcomes is open—the same good outcome can be produced by infinitely many different cognitive-method combinations, including large quantities of random factors, external contingencies, and uncontrollable variables. To confirm "my method" as the unique or primary cause of success, one must enumerate and causally exclude all other possible factors. The logical form of this enumeration is:

> Success = f(my method, factor₁, factor₂, ..., factorₙ, random noise)

To derive the causal conclusion "my method → success," the arguer must demonstrate that when all other factors are controlled to zero or constant, variation in my method independently produces variation in success. But n is infinite—there always exists "the next candidate factor you haven't excluded." Infinite enumeration is incompletable; therefore causal exclusivity is unprovable.

This logical proposition finds its clearest empirical support in highly professionalized domains. S&P Dow Jones Indices annually publishes the SPIVA (S&P Indices Versus Active) report tracking actively managed fund performance against benchmarks. Per the 2023 SPIVA U.S. Year-End Scorecard [5]: over the past year, approximately 60% of U.S. large-cap active funds underperformed the S&P 500; extending to ten years, approximately 87% underperformed; at fifteen years, over 92% underperformed. More critically, the SPIVA Persistence Scorecard shows that among funds ranked in the top 25% in one year, the proportion maintaining top-25% ranking over the next four years does not differ significantly from random distribution—top managers' skill signals are completely submerged in statistical noise after three years. If "correct methods" could sustainably produce excess returns over the long term, you would observe statistically significant persistence differences between benchmark-beating and benchmark-trailing managers. You do not. Fund manager "success" is a statistical event, not a causal event. Their material returns are real, but the causal attribution "investment method → excess returns"—without a control group—is statistically indistinguishable from randomness.

**Second: No Experimental Control Group.**

Social events lack laboratory-condition control groups—you cannot run the same society twice under identical initial conditions, applying a variable in one run and not the other. A successful person's "success path" therefore naturally lacks a control group: no one knows whether, had they adopted entirely different methods, the outcome would have been better, worse, or the same.

This absence is not a data-collection insufficiency—it is determined by the unidirectionality of time: the same individual at the same moment cannot repeat the same decision. You can only walk one path. The path you did not walk (your counterfactual control) does not exist in the material layer. Without a control group, causal inference cannot logically be established.

Nassim Nicholas Taleb, in *Fooled by Randomness* (2001) [6], provided a thought experiment demonstrating how absurd success attribution can become without a control group: assume 10,000 fund managers, each with a completely random investment strategy (coin flips). After one year, approximately 5,000 profit and 5,000 lose. After ten years, purely due to randomness, approximately 10 will have profited for ten consecutive years. These 10 will be profiled in financial magazines as "genius investors"—and their strategies were coin flips from start to finish. In sufficiently large samples, randomness will inevitably produce tail samples that appear to be "genius." "Consistent multi-year outperformance" itself proves nothing about methodological validity—it only proves a sufficiently large sample size.

Two classic cases from the success industry further corroborate this logic. Napoleon Hill, author of *Think and Grow Rich* (1937) [7]—which sold over 100 million copies worldwide—claimed to have interviewed hundreds of the most successful entrepreneurs, including Andrew Carnegie, Henry Ford, and Thomas Edison, extracting from them "laws of success." Material-layer audit reveals: Hill claimed to have had a three-day meeting with Carnegie in 1908, but Carnegie's biographers and schedule records contain no evidence supporting this meeting; *The New York Times* book reviewers estimated that fewer than 10 of the hundreds of interviews Hill claimed may have actually occurred. More damningly: Hill ran his own businesses using his own methods—and went bankrupt multiple times, ultimately relying on book sales and speaking fees for his livelihood. If "Think and Grow Rich" truly worked, the author himself should have been the first beneficiary—yet he could not even replicate his own success.

Robert Kiyosaki, author of *Rich Dad Poor Dad* (1997) [8]—which sold over 32 million copies worldwide—presents a similar case. Material-layer audit reveals: the central figure "Rich Dad" is almost certainly fictional—decades of investigative journalism have failed to locate any Hawaiian tycoon matching Kiyosaki's description; Kiyosaki's company Rich Global LLC filed for bankruptcy protection in 2012; a 2020 *New York Times* investigation reported that over 90% of Kiyosaki's wealth came not from the investment methods "Rich Dad" taught him, but from selling books and giving speeches—he earned far more teaching others to invest than from his own investments. This is not accidental—it is the material-layer truth of the success industry's business model: its profits come from selling the methodology, not from executing it.

**Third: Counterfactual Statements Are Unverifiable.**

All causal statements ultimately embed a counterfactual conditional: "if X had not occurred, Y would not have occurred." The truth condition of a causal statement is therefore not "whether X and Y simultaneously exist in this world"—it is "whether, in a possible world without X, Y would not occur."

But the truth of counterfactual conditionals requires cross-world comparison—comparing the actual world with a possible world that never existed. Material-layer verification—practice—can only be executed in the actual world. You cannot perform any verification operation on a world that never existed. Therefore, all counterfactual conditionals on which causal attributions ultimately depend are unverifiable in principle.

The three obstacles exhibit a progressive relationship: infinite enumeration addresses "too many factors," absence of control addresses "time does not go backward," and counterfactual unverifiability addresses "material verification cannot cross worlds." Each independently holds, and any one alone suffices to block the positive verification path of "success = correctness."

### 3.2 The Category Jump from Statistical Event to Causal Attribution

The above three incompletabilities point to a single conclusion: **success is a statistical event; causal attribution is the cognitive layer's narrative processing of a statistical event.**

Material outcomes are real—wealth indeed increased, status indeed rose, goals indeed were achieved. But the causal arrow "my method → these material outcomes"—without passing the above three tests—is not a real event; it is an internally coherent narrative within the cognitive framework. A category jump has occurred between them: from a material-layer fact (material outcomes exist) to a cognitive-layer proposition (my method is correct), crossing a non-existent causal bridge in between.

This is not "insufficient evidence"—it is "this kind of evidence does not exist in principle." Not a matter of degree; a matter of category.

### 3.3 Precise Formulation

> Success attribution is a **jump structure**—between material statistical outcomes and cognitive causal certification, no continuous, step-by-step traceable material conduction chain exists. Therefore, success is epistemically unverifiable.

## 4. Error Can Be Falsified—Material Traceability of Cognitive Defects

### 4.1 Step-by-Step Verifiability of Error Conduction Chains

Unlike success attribution, error attribution requires no jump.

"I stepped on a rake → the rake handle struck my face → I bled." Every step in this chain leaves observable material traces: the rake's trajectory can be reconstructed through mechanics, the shape and position of the facial wound match the dimensions of the rake handle, the blood's chemical composition can be identified as human blood. The entire causal conduction process is **continuous**—between one link and the next, there is no jump; each step can be independently disassembled and verified by independent observers.

### 4.2 Structural Contrast with Success Attribution

The fundamental difference between success attribution and error attribution is not between "difficult" and "easy"—it is between "requires counterfactual control" and "does not require counterfactual control."

Success attribution requires answering: "if I had used a different method, would the outcome be different?"—this is a counterfactual question, unverifiable.

Error attribution does not require answering a counterfactual question. It only requires answering: "from which cognitive defect did this material bad outcome originate, through which intermediate links was it conducted?"—this is a factual question; every step has material traces available for tracing.

Error itself is a fact in which "the counterfactual has already been executed." The arguer does not need to construct a virtual control group—the control group has already occurred in the material layer: you stepped on the rake (experimental group), you did not step on the rake (control group—a world without the blood on your face). The difference between the two is the causal verification itself.

### 4.3 Precise Formulation

> Error attribution is a **continuous structure**—every step of causal conduction from a cognitive defect to a material bad outcome leaves observable, traceable material traces in the material layer. Therefore, error is epistemically falsifiable.

### 4.4 Summary of the Asymmetry

Juxtaposing the conclusions of Sections 3 and 4 yields our core asymmetry:

> **Material success cannot verify cognitive correctness. Material failure can falsify cognitive error.**

This is not a philosophical position—it is a logical necessity of material causal structure. Positive verification requires a complete control group independent of the causal chain, and the material world does not provide such a control group. Negative verification requires no control group—it only requires tracing backward along the existing causal chain, and the material world provides material traces at every step of that chain.

## 5. Formal Foundation—Negative Feedback Dynamics

### 5.1 A Formal Framework from Social-Cognitive Dynamics

An independently developed mathematical framework [9] distinguishes two fundamental dynamical modes of social-cognitive interaction: uncoupled diffusion (governed by the diffusion equation ∂P/∂t = D∇²P—no restoring force, no damping, no convergence, zero resultant vector) and damped resonance (governed by the driven damped wave equation ẍ + 2ζω₀ẋ + ω₀²x = F(t)—damped oscillation converging toward material truth). The framework further shows, through laser physics isomorphism and Ising model solutions, that positive feedback channels are structurally blocked (diffusion without convergence) while negative feedback channels remain uniquely operable (damped convergence).

### 5.2 Translating Mathematics to Epistemology

Uncoupled diffusion = diffusion equation = scattered directions + no restoring force + zero resultant. Epistemically, this corresponds to the non-verifiability of success: when people exchange statements without material-layer access (the success industry being an exemplary case), the total system resultant vector is forever zero. Each "success case" is merely statement proliferation in a random direction—from the system's perspective as a whole, no net convergent vector pointing toward a "correct method" is produced.

Damped resonance = wave equation = damped oscillation + convergence toward the material layer. Epistemically, this corresponds to the falsifiability of error: when the material layer intervenes as an external damper ("I stepped on the rake, my face bled"—material feedback directly pierces the cognitive framework), each damped oscillation shaves off a portion of cognitive deviation. The direction of convergence is not "more correct"—it is "less erroneous." Because the convergence endpoint of the wave equation is x = 0 (cognitive deviation shaved to zero by material damping), not x = some positive value ("I found the correct direction").

### 5.3 Operational Translation

The operational translation of the asymmetry into a single sentence:

> Practice does not produce "knowledge of what is true"—it produces "knowledge of what is false."

The common saying "practice yields true knowledge" implies material feedback is bidirectionally symmetric—you can confirm what is right and confirm what is wrong through practice. The mathematical framework demonstrates this implication is structurally false. The framework structurally blocks positive feedback channels—success attribution has no operable convergence path (diffusion without restoring force, zero resultant). It uniquely preserves negative feedback channels—errors can be progressively reduced through material damped oscillation (wave equation, ζ > 0 → amplitude decays).

Therefore, the core operational mechanism is not verifying truth—it is eliminating error. It is a native negative-feedback engine.

## 6. Comparison with Popperian Falsificationism

### 6.1 Popper's "Falsification"

Karl Popper, in *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959) [10], proposed that for a proposition to qualify as scientific, it must satisfy "falsifiability"—there must exist a logically possible observation that, if it occurred, would declare the proposition false. For example, "all swans are white" is a scientific proposition because discovering one black swan would falsify it; "God exists" is not a scientific proposition because no possible observation could overturn it.

Popper excludes unfalsifiable propositions from science. He addresses a **proposition-demarcation problem**: which propositions qualify for entry into scientific discussion, and which do not.

### 6.2 Level Difference

This paper does not address the proposition-demarcation problem—it addresses the **inter-layer verification direction problem**: when a cognitive framework is tested against the material layer, does the direction of the test signal (from material to cognitive) possess an intrinsic asymmetry?

Popper's unit of argument is the "falsifiability" of an individual proposition. This paper's unit of argument is the structural difference between the "positive verifiability" and "negative verifiability" of a cognitive framework as a whole. The two operate at different levels:

- Popper: propositional logic level—"can this proposition be overturned by a single counterexample?"
- This paper: causal verification level—"is the positive causal attribution from a cognitive framework to a material outcome impossible in principle?"

### 6.3 Compatible But Non-Overlapping

The two are operationally compatible—both point toward "only error elimination counts as progress"—but derive through different paths and address different levels of analysis.

Popper's argument is rooted in formal logic: a universal proposition (∀x P(x)) cannot be verified by any finite set of positive instances, but can be falsified by a single counterexample (∃x ¬P(x)). This is a **proposition-demarcation** argument—it separates scientific from non-scientific propositions.

This paper's argument is rooted in causal structure: to verify the claim "my method caused my success," one would need a counterfactual control group (a parallel history in which the same person used a different method), which the material world does not provide. This is an **inter-layer verification direction** argument—it distinguishes positive causal verification (structurally impossible) from negative causal verification (structurally possible).

Popper did not address why positive verification fails at the causal-structural level. His framework shows that a universal proposition cannot be confirmed by finite evidence. Our framework shows that the very act of attributing a material outcome to a specific cognitive cause requires an operation (counterfactual comparison) that the material world cannot support—not because evidence is finite, but because the required evidence (a parallel history) does not exist. This is a stronger claim than Popper's: it is not that evidence is "insufficient"—it is that the evidence type required for positive causal verification is **nonexistent in principle**.

## 7. Implications

### 7.1 The Definition of Progress

If only error is falsifiable and success is not verifiable, then "progress" cannot be defined as "accumulating verified correctness"—because nothing has been truly verified.

Progress can only be defined as **the cumulative amount of verified errors eliminated**.

A system's cumulative progress at time t can be expressed as: progress(t) = total errors eliminated − total errors newly introduced. The identifiability of "errors eliminated" depends on the system's material-layer feedback recording function—the more faithfully records reflect the material layer (truthfully recording failures and their causal conduction chains), the higher the error elimination rate, and the greater the rate of progress.

### 7.2 The Direction of Learning

The above definition has a direct implication for learning: **the effective object of learning is not the methods of the successful—it is the failure chains of the failed.**

The causal connection between a successful person's methods and their success is unverifiable in principle. You cannot confirm whether "learning their methods" will bring you success—because no one knows how they would have fared in the control group. But a failed person's failure is continuous—every step from decision error to material bad outcome is traceable. You do not need to believe anyone's narrative; you only need to trace their material conduction chain and mark that pit as forbidden territory.

This is not "learn from the lessons of failures"-style chicken soup—it is a learning-efficiency maximization strategy derived from an epistemic asymmetry. The dataset of the successful lacks the failure control group; the dataset of the failed naturally contains the complete causal conduction chain.

### 7.3 Operational Significance for Self-Reflection

What the framework provides to the individual is not a set of "how to succeed" methods—it is **a continuously operating reverse-verification engine**.

This engine does not help you confirm what is right—it helps you lock down what is wrong. Because only errors are real and verifiable. The perception of correctness is always temporary, conditional, awaiting the next test to be overturned.

Therefore, the criterion for measuring the effectiveness of an individual cognitive framework is not the absolute value of material achievements—it is a ratio: **to what extent can you identify and eliminate your own errors / how many errors have you made that you have not yet identified.**

Every negative-feedback cycle you invest in this ratio—every time you hit a material wall, confirmed that "this path leads nowhere," and marked that forbidden territory into your cognitive framework—is one instance of real, accumulable progress. Not because you "learned what is right"—because you "eliminated one pit you will never step into again."

## 8. Conclusion

We have demonstrated an asymmetry in the direction of verification: material success cannot verify cognitive correctness, but material failure can falsify cognitive error. The root of this asymmetry is the "success = correctness" root category error—a syntactic template conflating material statistical outcomes with cognitive causal certification. At the epistemic level, success attribution is a jump structure due to three impediments: infinite enumeration, absence of experimental control, and unverifiable counterfactuals. Error attribution is a continuous structure due to the step-by-step traceability of causal conduction chains. A mathematical framework from social-cognitive dynamics provides formal support for this asymmetry: the non-convergence of diffusion structurally blocks positive feedback channels; the damped convergence of the wave equation uniquely preserves negative feedback channels as the sole operable path.

The framework thus operates not as a "practice produces true knowledge" system—but as a "practice produces false knowledge" system. At the structural level, it blocks positive feedback and preserves only negative feedback—this is what distinguishes it from all existing cognitive frameworks.

The argument of this paper is confined to the epistemic level. Applications of this asymmetry at the scale of civilizational history—including quantitative demonstration of "civilizational progress = cumulative error elimination" and case analysis based on historical failure audit databases—are reserved for subsequent work.
